First proposed 1978 · Renewed Christmas MMXXVI

A Modest Proposal

on Ely Hill, Petaluma

For preventing the family of Ely Hill from being a burden to one another at Christmas, and for making them beneficial to the publick.

Sign below

It is a melancholy object to those who walk through the shops in December, to see the shelves crowded with things that nobody on Ely Hill requires. I shall now therefore humbly propose my own thoughts, which I hope will not be liable to the least objection.

The Precedent

This is not the first Modest Proposal. On November 26, 1978, after a Thanksgiving made unusually quiet by circumstances beyond their control, the K. Kortums sat down and did the arithmetic. The family was exchanging 114 gifts every Christmas. At that rate, they warned, the Kortums would soon have to rent a hall, and nobody would be in bed before four in the morning.

Their remedy was one gift given and one gift received, by names drawn from two hats,* with nobody drawing from their own household. The ballot was limited to yes or no.† The aim, in their words, was Christmas as “a celebration firstly, a time for gift-giving secondly.”

* The letter: “a drawing will be made to match a giver with a receiver.” So, one hat of givers and one of receivers.

† Somewhat suspiciously, the vote count was not recorded, and the ballots cannot be found.

Forty-eight years on, the Proposal is amended as follows.

This Year

  1. The Gift. Each of us gives one gift to one person, at a cost of no more than eighty dollars.
  2. The Book. Each of us gives one book to a different person. No price limit, the more used the better!

The Signing

On the rolls:

For the eyes of only

You have drawn

One gift, $80 at most, for
One book, for

A reminder: the draw is secret until Christmas. Tell no one, not even your spouse, whom you cannot have drawn.
